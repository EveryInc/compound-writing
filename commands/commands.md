# Compound Writing Workflows

## Orchestration

- `scribe` — Route an open-ended request to the smallest useful workflow.
- `emergent` — Compose a custom workflow when no single tool fits.

## Project And Context

- `setup-project` — Create `VOICE.md`, `STYLE.md`, `examples/`, and `drafts/` together.
- `onboarding` — Define or refresh voice and style rules.
- `save` — Capture a confirmed preference, lesson, or workflow improvement.

## Develop The Idea

- `brainstorm` — Surface promising raw material.
- `interview` — Draw out the thinking, stakes, and material behind an idea.
- `thesis` — Generate possible central claims.
- `promise` — Clarify what the reader should anticipate or receive.
- `outline` — Organize material into a workable structure.
- `hook` — Generate opening options.
- `transition` — Bridge sections or ideas.
- `analogy` — Explain a difficult concept concretely.
- `simplify` — Rewrite complex material in plainer language.

## Draft And Revise

- `draft` — Turn available material into a complete draft.
- `dev-edit` — Fix argument, structure, stakes, evidence, and payoff.
- `line-edit` — Revise sentences without flattening voice.
- `voice-check` — Diagnose voice drift and offer a closer revision.
- `ai-check` — Remove common machine-generated writing patterns.
- `tracks` — Remove scaffolding and process residue.
- `final-pass` — Make a publication-readiness judgment.

## Pressure-Test And Add A Lens

- `objections` — Surface reader resistance and counterarguments.
- `panel` — Synthesize several reviewer perspectives.
- `debate` — Let reviewers challenge one another across rounds.
- `asshole` — Attack weak claims from the least charitable position.
- `hemingway` — Demand economy.
- `hitchcock` — Review suspense and tension.
- `reader` — Trace the first-time reading experience and flag confusion, missing setup, and off-putting friction.
- `mom` — Test general-reader clarity.
- `sedaris` — Find specificity, humor, and self-implication.
- `sorkin` — Review pacing and momentum.
- `vonnegut` — Apply story fundamentals.

The full toolbox is available immediately. Describe the outcome and let `scribe` route it, or name a tool directly when you know what you want.
