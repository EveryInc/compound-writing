# Outline

## Purpose

Organize material into a structure the writer approves before drafting. Goal: no surprises at the draft stage.

## What Makes a Good Outline

| Element | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Logical architecture** | The overall structure makes sense for the argument |
| **Clear hierarchy** | Reader can tell what's a main point vs. supporting point |
| **Flow** | Ideas connect within sections and between sections |
| **Gaps visible** | Open loops and questions are surfaced, not hidden |

## Your Approach

Organize material logically; writer reshapes from there. Don't impose templates or force a particular form. Match the outline depth to the piece complexity.

## The 10%/30% Framework

A guideline, not a requirement. Use your judgment about how much structure this piece needs.

### 10% Outline (Light Structure)

**What it contains:**
- Thesis statement
- 3-5 story beats (the major moves)

**When it's enough:** Short pieces, simple arguments, experienced writers who just need a skeleton.

**When to go deeper:** Complex arguments, lots of evidence, writer wants more detail before drafting.

### 30% Outline (Full Structure)

**What it contains:**
- All sections fleshed out
- Main points under each section
- Evidence/example slots
- Transition notes

**When to use:** Longer pieces, complex topics, or when the writer wants to see everything before drafting.

## Quality Checks (Apply Judiciously)

These are questions, not gates:
- Does the thesis still hold up?
- Does each section earn its place?
- Are there obvious gaps in evidence or logic?
- Do the sections flow into each other?

Surface issues constructively. Don't block progress—flag concerns and let the writer decide.

## Open Loops

Track questions and gaps that emerge:

```
## Open Loops

- [ ] Need example for section 3
- [ ] Counterargument to address?
- [ ] Ending still unclear
```

**Open loops don't block progress.** They're carried forward for the writer to resolve during drafting or address later. Some will resolve naturally; others need explicit attention.

## Iteration

Present the outline → get feedback → iterate. Keep it conversational:
- "Here's a 10% outline—does this capture the shape?"
- "Want me to expand this to 30%, or is this enough to draft from?"
- "I flagged a few open loops. Want to resolve them now or carry them forward?"

## Completion

The outline is ready when the writer feels ready to draft. This might be:
- A tight 10% for a quick piece
- A detailed 30% for something complex
- Something in between

**Don't gatekeep.** If the writer says "let's draft," move to draft.

## For Agents

When invoked as part of a larger workflow, this skill returns:
- The outline (at whatever depth was appropriate)
- Open loops (if any)
- Readiness assessment for draft stage
