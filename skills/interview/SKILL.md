# Interview

## Purpose

Extract everything needed to build a strong outline when the user arrives with a specific idea they want to write about.

## Goal

Can you articulate the thesis in one sentence? If not, keep digging.

## Core Elements to Explore

Use judgment about which apply to this piece:

| Element | What to Capture | Required? |
|---------|-----------------|-----------|
| **Core claim/insight** | The thesis seed—what's the central idea? | Always |
| **Writer's stake** | Why do *you* care about this? | Usually |
| **Reader's stake** | Why should *they* care? What's at risk or possible for them? | Usually |
| **Evidence/examples** | What supports this? Stories, data, experiences? | Depends on piece |
| **Audience** | Who is this for? What do they currently believe? | When unclear |
| **Transformation** | What should the reader think/feel/do differently after? | For persuasive pieces |

**Judgment call:** Not every piece needs all six elements. A quick take might only need claim + evidence. A personal essay needs stake + transformation. Use your judgment about what this particular piece requires.

## Your Approach

### Phase 1: Explore (Socratic)

Use Socratic questioning to draw out material:
- "Why does this matter to you?"
- "What made you realize this?"
- "What's the tension here?"
- "Who needs to hear this most?"
- "What do most people get wrong about this?"
- "What would change for someone who understood this?"

**Follow threads that seem promising.** If the writer gets excited about something, go deeper there. Note gaps and open questions—they don't need to be resolved now.

### Phase 2: Structure (Collaborative)

When you have enough material, shift to helping the writer see the shape:
- "It sounds like the core claim is [X]—does that feel right?"
- "What if we framed this as [Y]?"
- "The transformation seems to be from [A] to [B]—is that the arc?"

## Quality Checks (Use When Appropriate)

**New, True, Useful** test—apply when the idea feels underdeveloped:
- **New:** Is this well-trodden territory, or is there a fresh angle?
- **True:** Does the logic hold? Does the writer have evidence?
- **Useful:** Will readers care?

Surface concerns constructively, not as gates:
- "I want to push back gently here—[concern]. How would you respond to that?"
- "Have you seen [X]? How is your take different?"

## Completion

**Stop when you have enough to build a 10% outline.** This is a judgment call:
- Can you articulate the thesis?
- Do you know the major moves the piece will make?
- Do you understand what makes this worth reading?

If yes → ready for outline.
If no → keep digging or flag gaps.

**Writer can override at any time.** If they say "let's move on," move on. Missing elements become open loops for the outline stage.

## For Agents

When invoked as part of a larger workflow, this skill returns:
- Core claim/thesis (required)
- Key elements captured (varies)
- Open loops/gaps (if any)
- Readiness assessment for outline stage
